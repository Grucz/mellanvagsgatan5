Buying alcohol (Systembolaget)

In Sweden, alcohol above 3.5% is sold only in the state-run shops called Systembolaget. There is one at Frölunda Torg.

You must be 20 to buy alcohol at Systembolaget. The legal age to drink at bars and restaurants is 18.

History of the house

A glimpse into the past of Järnbrott and the "own home" movement.

The "own home" movement (Egnahemsrörelsen)

The movement emerged in Sweden around 1900, as a response to the rapid urbanisation, overcrowding and social divides that came with industrialisation. The state, the municipalities and employers began to see the private house with a garden as a way to improve living conditions for workers and lower-level civil servants.

Through favourable loans, affordable land and standardised house types, many more people could own their home. The idea was never only about housing — it was also about independence, responsibility and stable family life.

Järnbrott & this house

In Gothenburg, "own home" construction took off during the interwar years and continued after the Second World War. Järnbrott is a clear example: building started here in the 1930s and carried on into the 1950s.

The house you are staying in was built in 1952, along with all the similar houses around it. There were three house types to choose from. Families were allocated a plot, then decided how much help they wanted from local carpentry firms and how much they would build themselves.

Most of the houses here were largely built by the very people who bought the plots and went on to live in them.

A picture of the house in its original state hangs above the freezer in the basement.
